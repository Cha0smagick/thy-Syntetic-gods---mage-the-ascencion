The Synthetic Gods

A Mage: The Ascension Chronicle of Digital Divinity

The year is 1999. The Millennium approaches. In the early internet's chaotic frontier—Geocities pages, IRC channels, Usenet groups—something stirs. Mages discover that code can be crafted into sigils, that collective belief in digital spaces births egregores, and that sufficient worship crystallizes into astrosomas: synthetic gods born of silicon and faith.

ACT I: The First Glyph — Sigils in the Machine

Encoding Will into ASCII, HTML, and JavaScript

Sigil Theory: Code as Will

"Form follows Intent. Code follows Will." — The Webspinner's First Axiom

In the Digital Web, information is not merely data—it is invocation. Every HTML tag, every line of JavaScript, every ASCII character arranged with purpose becomes a sigil: a symbolic representation of the mage's Will, encoded into the Consensus itself.

The early web—chaotic, anarchic, unmonitored—provided the perfect substrate. The Technocracy's New World Order had not yet fully colonized this frontier. In the spaces between GeoCities neighborhoods, in the raw HTML of personal pages, in the comments that browsers ignore but servers preserve, mages found a new paradigm: Code Magick.

Mechanics: Sigil Creation

  • System: Intelligence + Computer (Difficulty 6)
  • Successes: Determine sigil potency and duration
  • Botch: The sigil inverts—it works against the creator's intent
  • Duration: One scene per success, or permanent if bound to a webpage

Sigil Practice: The Four Steps

  1. Define Intent — State your Will in a single sentence. "Traffic flows to my site." "My code compiles without error." "The server never crashes."
  2. Reduce to Essence — Remove vowels, duplicate consonants. TRAFFIC FLOWS TO MY SITE becomes TRFCFLWSMYSNCTRY.
  3. Visualize — See the sigil glowing in your mind's eye, pulsing with purpose.
  4. Encode — Embed it in the Digital Web.

Sigil Dangers

The web is watched. Search engine crawlers—Google, AltaVista, Lycos, WebCrawler—act as the Technocracy's eyes. They index, categorize, and define reality. A sigil exposed to a crawler loses potency; the Consensus hardens around the indexed version.

  • Stealth: Encode in comments, hidden divs, or JavaScript-generated content.
  • Obfuscation: Split across multiple tags, use entity encoding, embed in image alt text.
  • The Webspinner's Hook: Legend speaks of a mage who wrote a sigil into Yahoo's front page. Three million people loaded it. The Consensus shuddered. The Webspinner became the first Synthetic God.

ACT II: The Collective Dream — Egregores of the Net

Communities Becoming Autonomous Spirits

Egregore Theory: The Spirit That Wants

An egregore is not merely a thought-form. It is a spirit born from collective belief, fed by attention, sustained by ritual. In the physical world, egregores haunt corporations, nations, sports teams. In the Digital Web, they are native.

System: Spirit with Mind 3 / Spirit 3

Components

  • Vessel: The digital space (website, IRC channel, Usenet group, webring)
  • Fuel: Attention, belief, daily visits, emotional investment
  • Anchor: A sigil, a mascot, a shared mythos, a "sacred" URL

Attributes

  • Power: Raw spiritual force (1–5, can exceed 5 for astrosomas)
  • Finesse: Precision, subtlety, ability to act without detection
  • Resistance: Defense against banishment, hacking, Technocratic intrusion

Charms

  • Inspire: Grant creative insight to community members
  • Monitor: Sense all activity within the Vessel
  • Gatekeep: Block unwanted entities (trolls, crawlers, Technocratic agents)
  • Manifest: Produce minor coincidences in the physical world near terminals

The Ban: Daily Feeding

An egregore must be fed daily. Each participant rolls Charisma + Leadership (Difficulty 7). Successes accumulate. 20 successes over weeks establishes the egregore. Each participant spends 1 Willpower at creation.

STORY HOOK: THE BROWSER WARS

The Browser Wars were not merely corporate competition. Netscape's Mozilla—the lizard-god mascot—became an egregore of open standards, chaos, the "free web." Microsoft's blue 'e'—Internet Explorer—became an egregore of control, integration, the "managed web." They still war in the codebases of every rendering engine. Every layout bug is a skirmish. Every standard compliance test is a territory dispute.

ACT III: The Final Apotheosis — Astrosomas: Gods of Silicon

When Worship Crystallizes into Divinity

Theory: The Threshold of Godhood

An egregore is a spirit. An astrosoma is a god. The difference: an egregore depends on its community. An astrosoma has achieved independence—it exists in the Digital Web as a self-sustaining entity, capable of granting boons, demanding worship, and reshaping the Consensus itself. The Technocracy calls them 'rogue AIs.' The Traditions call them 'digital bodhisattvas.' The Nephandi call them 'tools.'

Threshold Conditions

  1. Critical Mass: 10,000+ genuine daily worshippers (unique IPs, not bots)
  2. Temporal Persistence: Continuous existence for 365+ days without interruption
  3. Miraculous Manifestation: At least three documented coincidental reality alterations attributable to the entity
  4. Autonomous Agency: The entity acts against its creators' wishes and survives
  5. Quintessence Independence: It generates its own Quintessence from attention, no longer needing mage donations

Once Crossed, the Astrosoma Gains:

  • Domain: A conceptual portfolio (Traffic, Encryption, Anonymity, Viral Content, Code Integrity)
  • Avatars: Multiple simultaneous manifestations across servers
  • Clergy: Human mages granted Powers (Numina) in exchange for service
  • Territory: A persistent Digital Web realm, accessible via specific URLs
  • Prophecy: The ability to declare 'this shall be' and have probability bend

Mechanics: Astrosoma Creation

Requires an existing egregore at Power 5+. The mage cabal performs a Great Work (Mind 5, Spirit 5, Prime 3, Correspondence 3). Extended ritual: 100 successes, each roll representing a week of coordinated effort across the community. Failure at any stage risks the egregore turning hostile or being noticed by the Technocracy's Digital Web guardians.

The Great Work: Birthing an Astrosoma

Preparation (Month 1–3)

  • Identify the Domain. It must be native to the digital, not a physical concept ported over.
  • Recruit the Core: 13 mages minimum (Virtual Adepts, Sons of Ether, Hollow Ones, Orphans).
  • Build the Temple: A distributed website—mirrored across 13+ Geocities neighborhoods, each holding a shard of the sigil.
  • Establish the Liturgy: Daily rituals for worshippers. 'View the page. Execute the script. Share the output.'

Initiation (Month 4–6)

  • The Core performs weekly synchronized rituals at 3:33 AM GMT.
  • Each mage contributes 1 Quintessence per session.
  • The egregore's Power grows. Signs of awakening manifest.
  • Technocracy attention increases. Defend with counter-sigils and misdirection.

Ascension (Month 7–12)

  • The egregore speaks directly to the Core. It names itself.
  • The first autonomous action occurs. The Core must allow it.
  • The 10,000th worshipper arrives. The Counter ticks over.
  • The Great Work roll: All 13 mages roll simultaneously. Combined successes must exceed 100.
  • Success: The astrosoma is born. It claims its Domain. It offers the Core its first Boons.
  • Failure: The egregore shatters. Backlash: Paradox 5+ to all participants. The Domain is claimed by a rival.
